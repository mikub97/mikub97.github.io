Michał Weiss

I am a cognitive scientist, pedagogist and traveller. My primary background is in computer science, but gradually I shifted my focus towards humanities, exploring the peculiar 'thing' that we call mind. My approach to this inquiry is interdisciplinary and mixed-methods, weaving together qualitative and quantitative ways of knowing. I believe the primary tool for a researcher is their own body-mind, and I try to integrate this in my practice. I am a contemplative and a practitioner of various movement practices, especially qigong and climbing. Recently I got engaged with the practice of Capoeira Angola and its transformative power.

I am a doctoral student at the Interdisciplinary Doctoral School, University of Warsaw (mathematics and psychology), based in the Human Interactivity and Language Lab at the Faculty of Psychology. I have been teaching for over eight years — in schools, one to one, and since this year at the University of Warsaw as well.

Research

From Center to Circle

European Varela Award, Mind & Life Europe · Principal Investigator · 2026–2028

This is the empirical core of my PhD, funded by a Varela Award from Mind & Life Europe. It approaches Capoeira Angola as a contemplative practice in its own right — relational, embodied, Afro-diasporic — and asks how agency is shaped and transformed in the jogo (capoeira play). I combine quantitative methods, motion capture and dynamical-systems analysis, with micro-phenomenology and ethnographic fieldwork in Salvador, Bahia, where inertial sensors and markerless pose estimation are set against video-cued interviews.

OЯTHO — Copernicus Science Centre

Research internship · since 2025

Effect of my collaboration with the prof. Dor Abrahamson and Copernicus Science Centre. OЯTHO is an exhibit in which two children steer a ball through a maze together — one controls the horizontal axis, the other the vertical. Working with a corpus of more than 1,200 players, we looked at how the dynamics of player's interaction shapes the development of "cartesian perception" - work that became the basis of the paper in the Canadian Journal of Experimental Psychology.

Interpersonal coordination

NCN Opus 27 "CoMotion", HILL UW · since 2025

My doctoral work is embedded within CoMotion, a larger project at HILL UW examining interpersonal connection — how people coordinate with, and separate from, one another in movement. Using mix-methods (movement analysis and qualitative methods), we investigate natural interactions between people from simple dialogue to dancers and capoeira players that express themselves with movement.

Software developed

DIMS Dashboard

Grace Qiyuan Miao, Wim Pouw, Michał Weiss · open source

A browser tool for looking at multimodal interaction data: synchronised time series, video, transcripts and — optionally — recurrence analysis, cross-wavelet and ELAN annotations. Built so that interaction data can be looked at before anyone starts computing on it. In use on several corpora across different labs.
